## D-208 — 7-Eleven receipts become a domain of their own: a reader, three tables, backup v8, and the coverage tripwire that was missing all along

- Date: 2026-09-22 / 2026-09-23
- Status: **Reader shipped as `02b2ff5` and pushed. Storage (migration 027), backup v8 (migration 028) and the coverage tripwire are built and validated locally.** Nothing hosted; no `db push`.
- Context: the owner asked for 7-Eleven receipts to be captured, itemized and reported on — "kinda like what we did with bank slips" — explicitly not as a separate app. He granted reading his real receipts and his real ledger for the measurements below.
- **The decision the whole feature rests on: a receipt is never money.** Every payment route already lands in the ledger on its own — a per-purchase TrueMoney pull, a wallet top-up, or a reimbursement — so a receipt contributing to totals would double-count in all three. Receipts itemize money already recorded, and receipt statistics are a separate lens beside the ledger rather than a second ledger inside it. **This is what keeps the exact-money, append-only and audit invariants untouched by the feature**, and it is why an unmatched receipt costs nothing: the totals are already correct without it. The owner confirmed this was his intent; the alternative — receipt spend appearing in monthly totals — was raised and not taken.
- **Measured against the real ledger (counts and descriptions only, nothing recorded).** The SCB account carries a substantial run of outgoing `SIPS TRUE MONEY` rows, a majority of them **not round figures** — the evidence that TrueMoney pulls per purchase rather than only on top-up. Round figures also appear and are the genuine top-ups; both models are in use at once.
- **The matching rule is fail-closed, and the case that proves it was the owner's own.** One measured receipt had no TrueMoney row at all — a third party's wallet paid it against his member number and he reimbursed by PromptPay immediately. An unrelated-looking PromptPay row of the same amount sat about a minute away, and amount-and-time matching alone would have taken it. It happened to be the economically correct row, which is **luck, not a rule**. So the automatic path considers only rows naming `TRUE MONEY`, requires an exact amount and a bank row at or after the receipt time within a lag window, refuses on two candidates, and treats unmatched as normal rather than an error. A **manual link** covers the rare case. **The lag window is still unmeasured** — two confirmed matches, at zero and roughly forty-five minutes — and is the remaining gate on the matching half.
- **The three forms are complementary, not ranked**, and this was learned the expensive way. The full tax invoice carries the only untruncated item names and the only VAT breakdown, and it alone prints **no payment method, no time, no unit count, and no `TID#`/`R#`**. Payment method is exactly what the matching rule keys on, so **a full invoice alone can never answer the matching question**. An upgrade therefore merges per field and never replaces per document, and a checksum a form cannot support is recorded **inapplicable, not passed**.
- **Ten of the sixteen reader corrections came from measuring the documents; none came from the tests.** An invented fixture validates the grammar that invented it, so the tests and the code agreed with each other while both disagreed with reality. The largest errors were modelling the full invoice as *extending* the condensed tail when it *replaces* it, and missing that discounts, promotions and merchandise share one line shape told apart only by name — the first draft read every discount as a positive item, which would have failed the net checksum on any receipt carrying one. **Recorded as the standing caution for the rest of this feature.**
- **Three apparent reading defects were extraction artifacts, not properties of the PDFs** — reordered Thai vowel and tone marks, interleaved columns, stray in-word spaces — all from layout-reconstructing extraction, which sorts glyphs by x-position while Thai combining marks are zero-width and sit above their consonant. In raw reading order all three vanish, confirmed under `pdfjs-dist` and independently under PyMuPDF. **One real defect remains and is fully recoverable**: the full invoice's font maps sara aa to `U+0006` (163 occurrences; no other control character at all), repaired by two deterministic replacements. **So there is no Thai normalizer and no new dependency**, and the PDF path needs no OCR, no Vision and nothing leaving the device — the only capture path in this app that reads a document without either. A space repair was refused: all nine space-after-a-combining-mark occurrences measured were legitimate word boundaries.
- **Storage is parsed data only** (the owner's choice), following D-050's reasoning for slips: the file already lives in his own storage. No bytes, no object storage, no file digest. Three tables — `receipts`, `receipt_items`, `receipt_discounts` — and no `merchants` table, since a single merchant does not pay for one and a second would be a second contract (D-031). **No matching table**, because the lag window is unmeasured and designing it now would be guessing.
- **`capture_receipt` follows `capture_slip`'s transaction discipline** — the ledger-mutation advisory lock, an audit event, and a `mutation_sequences` bump — established by reading that function rather than assuming. A backed-up owner table that skips the bump can be captured mid-write, because the backup's consistency check reads that sequence.
- **Three write rules, deliberately different from each other.** Receipt scalars **merge** (`coalesce`), so a later source's null never clears a stored value. Money fields that **disagree refuse** rather than merging — two readings of one purchase must agree, and picking a winner would launder a misread. Items and discounts are **replaced together as a unit**, and only by a source of equal or higher rank (`complete` worth 2, `full` worth 1), so a truncated screenshot can never overwrite a complete PDF's item list; `display_name` is the owner's own and survives by position. The asymmetry is recorded in the migration because it looks arbitrary otherwise.
- **The tripwire is the finding worth keeping.** Splitting the work predicted a failing backup-coverage gate between the two passes. **It did not fail — because no such gate existed.** Coverage was enforced only by hardcoded name arrays inside `restore_backup` and the field list in `export_backup_snapshot`, so any future migration could add an owner-bearing table, the backup would silently not carry it, and every test would still pass — while `SPEC.md` gate 6 promises "a backup covers **every** table holding owner records: one file restores the whole ledger, or the property is lost." That is the same class of silent staleness D-105 caught in this area once before. `supabase/tests/016_backup_coverage.sql` now queries the **live schema** for every `public` table with an `owner_id` and asserts each is either backed up or on an explicit, commented exclusion list, and asserts the inverse so a renamed or dropped table is caught too. **Red-proved**: removing `receipt_discounts` from the list fails it, restoring it passes. Its own residual limit is recorded in the file: the SQL list is a fourth hand-maintained mirror, so it catches schema-versus-list drift but not a table added to the SQL mirror and forgotten in the TypeScript.
- Gate (verified independently, not on the agents' report): `pnpm supabase:reset` on 28 migrations; **Vitest 1015 passed / 7 skipped across 46 files**; **pgTAP 16 files, 441 tests, PASS** including the new `015` (36) and `016`; `tsc` clean; `eslint` 0 errors (the 2 pre-existing `app/transactions-view.tsx` warnings remain); `pnpm build` clean; `check:docs --strict` clean. **The v7-into-v8 restore pair genuinely ran against the live recovery destination** (4.1s) rather than skipping — the one skip in that file is the intentional inactive fallback. `/code-review high` on the reader found one confirmed defect, fixed and red-proved: the bare-quantity line shape matched the full invoice's printed head-office address block and read it as merchandise of quantity 313.
- **Backup contract moves to v8, reads v2 … v8**, with the three kinds appended parent-before-child because the restore inserts in list order. `SPEC.md` gate 6 corrected in place.
- **A subagent refused this task once, correctly**, on the first attempt at the migration: the spec had said "merge per field, never replace" while item rows plainly have to be replaced somehow, and it returned that contradiction rather than guessing at money and identity semantics. The rules above are the resolution.
- Not done: no route, no UI, no screenshot/OCR path, no matching, no statistics. `/security-review` not run. The `capture_receipt(jsonb)` wire contract is **provisional** — it was defined with no caller to constrain it and should be revisited when the upload route is built. Two fail-closed reader limits stand, recorded in `PLAN.md` task 56.
