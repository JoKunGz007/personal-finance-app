# Private Ledger specification index

Last reviewed: 2026-08-12

This file is the stable entry point for project requirements. It summarizes only the cross-cutting contract and links to the detailed specifications that own the exact behavior.

## Product objective

Private Ledger is a local-first, single-owner application that converts a supported bank statement — Krungthai, SCB, or KBANK — into a reconciled, reviewable ledger without exposing the PDF, its password, or private financial data. Bank slips are images, captured through the app as provisional entries the statement later confirms (D-050, `PLAN.md` tasks 20–22); the OCR-first framing of D-037 is superseded, and cash receipts are entered by hand. **Slip capture is built as of 2026-07-30** (D-056, migration 011): a slip's identity comes from its QR — bank and transaction reference, checked against the payload's own CRC — while the amount, date and counterparty are the owner's, typed and confirmed. A slip is never authoritative and never carries an account; the statement stays the authority. **No slip image is stored**, in the database or anywhere else.

**OCR reached the app on 2026-08-10 as an amount *finder*, not a pre-fill** (D-087, D-088, task 21 done). The engine — Thai only, self-hosted under `public/tesseract/`, loaded on demand and never from a CDN — is used to **locate** the amount and show it cropped and enlarged beside the field. The owner still types it. **No machine-read digit reaches a stored value**, which is the invariant the measurement forced: digits came back unstable about one time in fifteen across configurations, and at least one wrong figure passed the strict money grammar. Locating answers a strictly easier question and therefore covers more slips than reading would — 16 of the 23 real samples on the shipped engine version. Nothing on the device is stored to do it, the language model included.

**Slips are reconciled against statement rows as of 2026-08-01** (D-063, task 22 half done, read-time only). No layout prints the transaction reference the QR carries, so a match is inferred from bank, an amount equal to the minor unit, and the nearest date **within one day** (narrowed from three on 2026-08-05, D-064: the owner's banks post same-day, so the window is tolerance for the slip's clock and the bank's straddling midnight, not for a late posting) — and is made only when that pair is mutually unique, so two identical payments refuse rather than guess. A matched pair is shown as one row, the statement's, which is what lets the ledger's totals count each payment exactly once. **The owner's override and undo are stored as of 2026-08-05** (D-067, migration 012): a decision names the statement row or says there is none, it is audited and revisioned, its history is append-only, and it is applied **before** the automatic rule so an explicit decision can never lose to a proposal. A statement row can be claimed by at most one slip, enforced in the database rather than only by the reader. **The decision is reachable from the app as of 2026-08-07** (D-068): the ledger row carries the control, `PUT /api/v1/slips/[id]/match` is the only caller of the RPC, and the stored decisions travel on the same response as the slips they are about. What an override may assert is deliberately narrow — the same bank and the same amount to the minor unit — so it resolves ambiguity and rejects a wrong pairing without being able to declare two different sums one payment.

**Cash and corrections complete task 22 as of 2026-08-10** (D-084, migrations 013 and 014, on the synthetic project only). A cash payment is a ledger fact with no statement behind it: its own table rather than a statement row, no account, no printed balance, and **never matched to anything** — there is no row it could collapse onto, now or later — so it is counted apart from provisional slips rather than folded in with them. What the owner typed on a slip or a cash entry is **correctable; what the QR carried is not** — a slip's bank and reference are absent from the correction overlay entirely, because a correctable identity would let one slip be re-typed into another's. Neither record is ever edited: both tables are append-only, a correction is a separate overlay with an append-only history, and both figures survive a backup. The **amount in force is resolved once, at the edge of the read path**, so the match rule, the totals and the row cannot disagree about which figure is real — migration 014 exists because the database's own guard once compared a slip's original amount against a statement row, refusing correct pairings and accepting wrong ones.

**A bank's LINE push notification can be captured and reconciled as of 2026-08-13** (D-098 through D-102, migration 016, `PLAN.md` task 27, which is complete). The table, its identity rule and its write path landed first (D-098), then the printed grammar (D-099), the reader (D-100), the capture form with its route (D-101), and finally the ledger view and the reconciliation (D-102). **The printed balance is what a card reconciles on, and it is the field a slip has never had**: it breaks the tie between two rows of the same amount that the slip rule refuses outright, and a card that fits on account, amount and date while contradicting every such row's balance **refuses to pair** rather than pairing on what agreed. It is a tie-breaker and a fail-closed cross-check, **not identity** — measured equal to the statement row's on 6 of 6 real cards, but none captured while a hold was outstanding, and *available* and *remaining* balances diverge exactly then. A card's pairing is recomputed on every read: it has no stored match decision and no correction overlay, each of which needs its own table. Three further properties are worth stating here because they are contracts rather than implementation. **The reader locates and never reads**: a card stores four digit-bearing fields — amount, balance, timestamp, account digits — and all four are typed by the owner, which is D-087's rule binding wider than it does on a slip. **The channel comes from the owner, not the card**: SCB Connect and KBank Live print an identical incoming title, so nothing in the card body distinguishes them, and the account-digit mask differs between the two. The route is the only layer that checks the printed digits resolve to the chosen account — the database stores that pairing rather than checking it, and the table is append-only. Not every payment produces the QR-bearing e-slip task 20 reads; when one does not, the bank pushes a card and the owner screenshots it, several times a week. A card is neither a slip (no reference, no QR, and a fingerprint is idempotent rather than tamper-evident) nor a cash entry (cash is never matched to a statement row and a card will be). Identity is computed in the database over the account, channel, kind, amount, timestamp and **printed balance** — the balance being what tells two payments of the same amount at the same minute apart. The card is bound to one of the owner's accounts at capture, and **the year and account-digit rules are per-layout, never global** (`lib/notification-card.ts`), because one layout's convention applied to all three is D-031 exactly. The backup moves to **v6** in the same migration, since a table the export does not carry breaks gate 6 the afternoon it exists.

**Committed data is no longer invented only.** On 2026-07-28 the owner imported one real KBANK statement — 55 rows across 2 pages, cross-checked against the bank's printed totals — into the local ledger, and on 2026-07-29 thirteen more followed (D-054). The fifteenth was refused that day for a balance chain its printed totals could not explain, and imported once the cause was found to be printed order rather than a reader defect (D-055) — taking the ledger to **1,465 rows across 15 batches and three accounts**, which is every statement held. The import happened after the recovery gate passed (D-044) and account creation made binding possible (D-045, D-047). Fixtures, seeds and tests remain invented and always will; what changed is the database, and every constraint below now protects real records rather than synthetic ones. Hosted Supabase, OAuth setup, Vercel, deployment, commits, and pushes still require separate explicit authorization. Local real-statement smoke tests were authorized on 2026-07-25 under stated conditions and were run ten times by the owner, reading a statement end to end; every finding came from on-device masked diagnostics, so no real value entered the repository. Direct access to `private-statements/` was granted the same day under an **invoke, don't read** boundary, and `docs/FIXTURE_POLICY.md` is amended accordingly (D-035) — a masking harness may be run against a statement, and the statement itself may not be read. That directory remains closed; since 2026-07-28 real statements may be read as the password-free copies in `shared-statements/` (D-049), which does not change what may be *written*: fixtures stay invented and no real value is committed, quoted or screenshotted. See `PLAN.md` § Later authorization gates and `HANDOFF.md` § Standing authorizations before acting on any of it.

## Authoritative invariants

- Money is signed 64-bit integer minor units with explicit `THB`. JSON boundaries use canonical decimal strings; binary floating point is never authoritative.
- Source facts, components, provenance, artifacts, batches, overlay history, and audit events are append-only.
- Import, restore, category mutation, retries, and other replayable operations are idempotent or conflict explicitly.
- Dates use the documented statement meaning. Derived instants and reporting boundaries use `Asia/Bangkok`.
- The PDF and password remain inside the dedicated browser worker. Unknown or unvalidated layouts fail closed.
- PostgreSQL is a second enforcement boundary for ownership, reconciliation, immutability, audit, and recovery.
- Strong access requires the bound owner, `aal2`, and **one** verified TOTP factor — narrowed from two on 2026-08-11 (D-093, migration 015, superseding D-004), because reaching `aal2` only ever needed one and the second factor gated nothing while being one more thing to lose. This line said "two" until 2026-08-12 and was wrong for a day: `lib/owner-access.ts` exports `REQUIRED_FACTORS = 1` and `private.has_strong_owner_access` counts `>= 1`. An unverified or non-TOTP factor still counts for nothing.
- Backup freshness means confirmed custody of an encrypted restorable artifact, not merely successful snapshot generation.

## Current local acceptance gate

Before any real-data or hosted stage:

1. A clean local Supabase reset must apply every migration and synthetic seed.
2. Lint, strict TypeScript, unit/property tests, pgTAP, production build, Playwright, privacy checks, and accessibility checks must pass on the current source.
3. Synthetic PDF geometry fixtures must validate the exact supported parser contract before parser support is enabled.
4. Backup export, client encryption, acknowledgement, staged restore, and equality must be exercised end to end with canonical int64 boundaries and more than 1,000 rows.
5. No PDF bytes, passwords, tokens, real values, analytics, service worker caches, or third-party font requests may cross their documented boundary.
6. An encrypted backup must restore into an **empty, separately bound project** — one that never held the ledger and never knew its owner — with every owner and actor identity rebound, including identities embedded in jsonb. A backup covers **every** table holding owner records: one file restores the whole ledger, or the property is lost. Adding a table therefore changes the backup schema version, and **a version the owner still holds a backup in must stay restorable** — the current contract reads **v2, v3, v4, v5 and v6** and writes **v6** (D-056, D-067, D-098, migration 016). New owner data goes in a **new table and never a new column**, which is what keeps that promise cheap enough to keep forever (D-097). **On the synthetic and recovery projects only:** the hosted project is on migration 015 until 016 is pushed there, so the newest file the owner holds is a **v5** file, and `private-ledger-live` is frozen on 012 and its files are **v4**. Each version keeps its own table list rather than the newest being applied leniently to all, so widening what is accepted is not the same as relaxing it: a v3 manifest does not pass as v4, and a v2 payload carrying a `slips` key is still refused. **The v4-into-v5 pair is exercised as of 2026-08-10** (D-089) — a v4 file stages, chunks and commits into a 014 ledger, the tables it predates stay empty rather than being invented, rebinding is unweakened by the version gap, and the destination re-exports as the current version. That is the pair hosting used, since live writes v4 and the hosted project started on 014. Before that, v4 was covered by the client's schemas and by construction, never by a restore: only v2 (pgTAP) and a real v3-into-v4 (D-078, operational) had ever run. **The v5-into-v6 pair is exercised as of 2026-08-12** (D-098), the same way and for the same reason one step later: the owner's newest file is the v5 export from the hosted project, so that is the pair the next recovery uses, and it was proven before anything depended on it.

Status as of 2026-07-28 for SCB and KBANK: gate 1 passes with migrations 001–010, re-verified by a clean reset of `private-ledger-local` on 2026-07-28; gates 2 and 5 pass on the current source; gate 3 is met by the invented fixtures in `tests/fixtures/statement-layouts.ts`, which are also rendered into real PDFs and read through pdf.js in the browser suite — and is now **discharged for parsing** on both. On 2026-07-27 the owner read one real statement of each layout, each on the first attempt: 94 rows across 5 pages for SCB, and 55 rows across 2 pages for KBANK. The KBANK read is independently verified twice over — the masked dump of that document covers both its pages in full and contains exactly 55 transaction rows, and the bank's own printed counts and totals agreed with all of them.

Those reads established parsing only, and that limit is now fully lifted. The KBANK statement was imported on 2026-07-28 (D-047) and the ledger moved to `private-ledger-live` (D-048); thirteen more followed on 2026-07-29 (D-054) and the fifteenth on the same day once its refusal was diagnosed (D-055). **All fifteen genuine statements are in the ledger: 1,465 rows across 15 batches**, which equals the row total an independent harness read from the same fifteen documents — the reader and the ledger agree on every row. Reconciliation against real rows is exercised only in the small: the live ledger holds one real slip and no stored decision, and cash and corrections have never run against it at all, since live is on migration 012 and 013/014 are not authorized to reach it.

The app can create the account a statement needs, as of 2026-07-27 (D-045). Until then every account came from the seed and `public.accounts` had no write path at all, so a statement printing an unmatched suffix — which both real statements did — could be read and then bound to nothing. Account writes go through `public.mutate_account` alone; `authenticated` still holds no insert, update or delete on the table, and pgTAP asserts it.

**Gate 6 is discharged with a real artifact as of 2026-08-09** (D-078): the owner's own encrypted backup — schema v3, 4,451 rows across twelve tables — was decrypted and restored into a freshly recreated `private-ledger-recovery` bound to a different owner, arriving at every count the live ledger holds with no trace of the source owner anywhere, including inside `overlay_revisions.snapshot`. It was also the first **cross-version** restore ever exercised, a v3 file into a v4 ledger, which is what this gate's "a version the owner still holds a backup in must stay restorable" had asserted without demonstrating. The copy was discarded immediately afterwards. Gate 6 passes locally as of 2026-07-27 (D-044): a ledger exported from the primary project restores into `private-ledger-recovery`, a second project bound to a different owner, with every identity rebound — including one embedded inside `overlay_revisions.snapshot` that no column-level check reaches. That discharges `PLAN.md` § Later authorization gates item 3 locally. The app can now take and restore a real backup itself (D-046, `PLAN.md` task 14): the `/recovery` route — § Recovery / 04 on the single page until routing split the app on 2026-07-31 (D-061) — exports an encrypted `.plbak` and restores one into an empty ledger, proven by a browser test that destroys the ledger between the two halves.

On 2026-07-28 that machinery was used for its actual purpose rather than as a rehearsal: the real ledger moved into `private-ledger-live` by restoring its own backup into that empty project, verified by identical account-id, transaction-id and fingerprint digests across both projects (D-048). ~~**No hosted recovery has been rehearsed**~~ — **superseded on 2026-08-11, and not by a rehearsal but by the real thing** (D-094): the owner's v4 backup was restored into the hosted Supabase project, which writes v5, and verified against the destination database on every count with no trace of the source owner in any table. The ledger has lived there since. The app serving it has been hosted since 2026-08-12 (D-096).

Status as of 2026-07-25 for Krungthai: gates 1, 2, 4, and 5 pass against synthetic data — re-verified after the account-binding UI and route-wrapper coverage landed (D-021, D-022) — and gate 3 is met for the transaction grid and statement frame by the invented fixtures in `tests/fixtures/krungthai-layout-v1.ts` (DECISIONS D-015, D-016). Gate 3 remains only partly discharged in substance: the fixture geometry was invented rather than measured, so it validates the parser contract without establishing that a real Krungthai layout matches it. The authorized smoke tests are the only check of that. Ten attempts on 2026-07-25 found eleven defects between them (D-023 … D-032), and gate 3 is now **discharged for parsing**: a real statement opens, decodes, matches its bank signature and all seven column headings, states its currency, yields its frame, and parses all 233 of its rows across 12 pages through to the account-binding stage. The date format is confirmed. Gate 3 is now discharged for verification too: the reader cross-checks every import against the statement's own printed counts and totals and fails closed on disagreement (D-033), and on the real statement those counts sum to exactly the rows read while the totals close the balance chain. All three paths that were then unreached behind a real PDF — binding, the authenticated import path, and charset rejection — were reached in a real browser on 2026-07-25 once a flag-gated development sign-in existed to mint the `aal2` session (D-036). ## Explicit non-goals for the current stage

- No production deployment or hosted resource creation without separate explicit authorization at the time. **This rule is unchanged by having been used.** Hosting and Google OAuth were accepted as direction and sequenced as one move (D-051, `PLAN.md` task 19), then each hosted resource was asked for and granted separately: the Google client and the Supabase project on 2026-08-11 (D-092, D-094), the Vercel project and its deployment on 2026-08-12 (D-096). All of those grants are spent and none is standing — a further project, a custom domain, a second region or any new external service needs its own approval at the time.
- No real statement parsing or smoke test without the owner present. The 2026-07-25 grant has been exercised twelve times and is discharged for all three layouts; it is not a standing licence for unattended runs.
- No multi-user sharing, currency conversion, investment tracking, budgeting, or bank credential storage.
- No statement password in environment variables, logs, database rows, or backup metadata.
- No claim that a synthetic `.pldemo` preview is a restorable backup.
