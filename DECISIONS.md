## D-114 — Pre-fill is trialled rather than decided, and the statement is the independent check the usage stats cannot be

- Date: 2026-08-16
- Status: **The owner's decision, scoping `PLAN.md` task 34. Nothing built.** D-087 is not reversed here — it is put on trial with instrumentation, and the trial's result is what will reverse or confirm it.
- **The decision.** Asked to choose between keeping D-087 and adopting pre-fill, the owner chose neither: **build it, collect statistics on how it is actually used, and decide later on the evidence.** That is the same instinct that produced D-112 and D-113 — measure the thing rather than argue about it — applied one level up, to his own behaviour rather than to the engine's.
- **Why this is the right shape and not a dodge.** The case against pre-fill was never that OCR misreads digits; D-112 and D-113 measured that it does not, and that it fails visibly. The case against it is **rubber-stamping** — that a plausible value in a filled box gets confirmed without being read. That is a claim about a person, and no amount of engine measurement can settle it. It can only be observed.
- **The trap in the obvious statistic, named before it is collected.** The natural measure is how often the owner edits a pre-filled value. **A low edit rate is consistent with two opposite worlds**: the engine is right, or the owner has stopped looking. The statistic cannot tell them apart, and a later reading of it that forgets this would conclude "it works" from the evidence for "he stopped checking".
- **So the trial needs an independent verifier, and the ledger already has one: the statement.** A card pairs with its statement row on the **exact** amount, a one-day window and the printed balance. A wrongly pre-filled figure therefore **fails to pair** and surfaces as `AWAITING STATEMENT` — visibly, without anyone auditing anything. **The pairing rate of pre-filled cards against typed ones is the check the edit rate cannot be**, because it does not depend on the owner having looked. If pre-filled cards pair as often as typed ones did, the engine is doing its job; if they pair less, something is being confirmed unread.
- **What gets recorded is structure, never values**, matching what `capture_notification_card` already writes: which fields were offered a pre-filled value, and which of those the owner changed before submitting — field names and nothing else. No amount, balance, date or digit enters an audit row, and none may.
- **A migration is unavoidable and its cost is known.** The capture RPC builds its own audit detail from the card row and accepts nothing from the caller, so recording this means a new parameter — **migration 019**. It adds **no table and no column**, so the backup contract stays at **v7** and no file is stranded; D-097's rule is about new owner data, and a field name in an audit detail is not owner data. It also means the local projects go one ahead of the hosted one again, which is the ordinary cost of a migration and not a reason to avoid one.
- **The trial is reversible and the decision it feeds is not urgent.** Pre-fill can be removed by not filling; the audit rows it leaves are append-only and harmless. Nothing about this weakens the two checks that already stand: `readDirection` still refuses when the card's words and the amount's sign disagree, and the strict grammar still blanks a field rather than guessing at it.
- Evidence: D-087 (the decision on trial), D-112 and D-113 (the engine measurements that make a trial reasonable), D-102 (the pairing rule that supplies the independent check), migration 016 § `capture_notification_card` (the audit row that gains a parameter). `PLAN.md` task 34.

## D-115 — A garbled separator hid a label, not just a value, and the month-name reader that looked obvious was worth nothing

- Date: 2026-08-16
- Status: **Built and measured.** `PLAN.md` task 34 parts 1 and 2 are done; parts 3 and 4 are not. Extends D-113 with a second measurement pass over the same ten images under a read grant given that day.
- **What was built.** `lib/notification-card-prefill.ts`, ported from D-113's throwaway harness: repair characters carrying no value, **discard the repair whenever the digit sequence changed**, run the unchanged `parseThb`, return nothing rather than a guess. The form then offers those values into the four digit-bearing boxes and **remembers exactly what it offered**, so a change is detectable. The port reproduces D-113's four rates exactly — amount 17/19, balance 15/19, date 10/19, own account 11/19 — which is what establishes it as a port rather than a rewrite.
- **A defect in the harness the port inherited, found by a test rather than by review.** The harness tried every single token before any rejoined pair, so on a balance whose thousands comma had been split into two words the *fragment* parsed first and won — offering a tenth of the printed figure. That is a **wrong-but-plausible value**, the one outcome D-112 measured as absent and the whole design exists to prevent, and **the harness could not have caught it**: it never graded its own accepted values, which is precisely the column D-112 and D-113 both say they cannot fill in. Fixed by trying the join before the single at each position; over-joining is refused by the money shape, which requires proper thousands grouping. **The general lesson is the session's own: a measurement's instrument can carry a defect that its output cannot reveal.**
- **The month-name date reader was built, measured at zero, and removed.** KBank Live prints `d <thai-month-abbrev> yy hh:mm` rather than a slashed date, so teaching the module the month vocabulary looked like the obvious way to raise D-113's 53%. It filled **0 of the 2** KBank cards in the sample. The structural diagnosis, taken value-free: **no month token survived recognition at all**, the hour/minute separator was absent, and the line came back carrying a single four-digit run. **The grammar was never what was missing**, so the code came out rather than staying as a path that has never once succeeded. A test holds the refusal in place so adding it back is deliberate. Revisit with a cleaner input — LINE's own Capture (D-111) — and re-measure first. **n is 2**, which is the honest limit on this conclusion.
- **The finding that actually moved the number, and it was in the wrong module.** D-113 established that `/` comes back as `|` or `!` on essentially every card and treated that as a problem for a date's *value*. It is also a problem for the **anchor**: SCB Connect anchors its timestamp on the label `วันที่/เวลา` and KBank Live's outgoing title is `รายการโอน/ถอน`, and both carry a slash. A garbled slash therefore made the field **unfindable**, and the reader refused it as `LABEL_NOT_FOUND` — a refusal that reads as *this card prints no timestamp* when the truth is that one character of its label did not recognise. Of the 19 cards, **7 date refusals were the reader declining to locate the field** against 2 that reached the pre-fill and failed there, so the field's constraint was never where the work had been aimed.
- **Repairing the separator before matching moved date and time from 10/19 to 12/19 (53% → 63%)**, with amount, balance, own account and the card count all unchanged and no new layout ambiguity. Five of the seven remain refused, garbled some other way. The repair is safe for a reason stronger than the pre-fill's own guard: this text is used **only to match labels and titles**, never to read a figure and never to build a crop, so nothing it does can move a pixel the owner is shown or a digit they are offered. Asserted by a test that the crop is byte-identical with and without the misread.
- **What the form now does and deliberately does not.** It fills the four digit-bearing boxes, names them on screen so a filled box is not mistaken for a typed one, and holds two lists of **field names** — offered, and changed before submitting. It does **not** fill the direction control: `readDirection` compares the card's words against the owner's choice, and filling the owner's half would compare the image with itself and always agree. **A card that comes on screen replaces all four boxes**, including those the engine refused, because inheriting the previous card's figure beside this card's crop is the mismatch the `cropPass` guard already exists to prevent.
- **`tests/privacy.test.ts` was reversed, which is worth recording as an event rather than a diff.** It asserted that no card figure could be pre-filled — D-087 held in place by a test, exactly as intended. D-114 put that on trial, so the assertion now guards the two things that still must hold: a figure may reach a box **only** through the strict module, never through a parser written at the call site; and the audit lists are built by filtering the field-name constant, so a value cannot travel in one by construction.
- **Still not established, and unchanged since D-112:** whether an *accepted* figure is **correct**. That needs the card, and the card is the owner's to read.
- **An operational mistake worth carrying.** The first harness run used the harness config without naming a file, which re-ran D-113's harness too and **rewrote `.runtime/card-ocr-readings.tsv`**. The values are identical, but any marks the owner had made on it are gone — and marking that file is the one open way to establish correctness. Name the file when running anything under that config.
- Evidence: `lib/notification-card-prefill.ts`, `tests/notification-card-prefill.test.ts`, `lib/notification-card-ocr.ts` (`repairSeparators`), `tests/notification-card-ocr.test.ts`, `app/notification-card-capture.tsx` (`offerPrefill`), `tests/privacy.test.ts`. Both measurement harnesses were throwaway, gitignored and are deleted. D-087, D-112, D-113, D-114, D-102, D-031. `PLAN.md` task 34.

## D-116 — Migration 019 records the pre-fill as field names, and adds two optional payload keys rather than a parameter

- Date: 2026-08-16
- Status: **Written, applied locally, tested. Not on the hosted project.** `PLAN.md` task 34 part 3. Part 4 — the route passing the lists through — is **not built**, so nothing sends them yet. Taken after the owner reported a fresh backup and authorized the migration.
- **What it records, and the limit on how it may be read.** Two arrays of **field names** join the audit detail `capture_notification_card` already writes: which of a card's four digit-bearing fields the pre-fill offered, and which of those the owner changed before submitting. **This is the denominator for the trial, never its answer.** D-114's trap is worth restating where the data is produced: a low change rate is consistent with the engine being right *and* with the owner having stopped looking, and no reading of this column can separate them. What settles the trial is the pairing rate of pre-filled cards against typed ones (D-102), because a wrongly pre-filled figure fails to pair and surfaces as `AWAITING STATEMENT` without anyone auditing anything.
- **Structure, never values**, which is the same rule the capture event was already built to. A closed set of four names — `amount`, `balance`, `occurredAt`, `ownAccount` — and nothing else. A pgTAP test asserts neither stored list carries a digit, on the row rather than on the payload, because the row is what survives.
- **The decision that matters operationally: two optional payload keys, not a new parameter.** `capture_notification_card` has always taken a single `jsonb`, so this is `create or replace` on an unchanged signature — no overload, no ambiguity, and no window in which the deployed app calls a function that no longer exists. **Absent means an empty list rather than an error**, and that is the deployment-ordering rule rather than politeness: every push to `main` deploys (D-109), the app in production sends neither key until part 4 ships, and a migration that refused the old payload would break card capture from the moment it was applied until the app caught up. A pgTAP test holds exactly that case, named for the reason it exists.
- **The closed set is enforced in the database as well as at the route.** The route is not the last boundary and an audit row is append-only, so the checks live where they cannot be bypassed: an unknown name, a repeated name, a non-array, a non-string element, and a `changed` list that is not a subset of `offered` are each refused by their own message. The subset rule is the one worth naming — a field changed that was never offered is not a correction, it is a caller with a broken model of its own form, and it would inflate every rate computed from these rows.
- **No new table and no new column, so the backup contract stays at v7.** D-097's rule is about new owner data needing a new schema version; a field name inside an existing jsonb detail is neither new owner data nor a new relation, and `audit_events` is already carried by `export_backup_snapshot`. **Verified rather than argued**: portable recovery passes on all four named lines against a 019 ledger, `restores a v5 file into the current ledger` included, which is the pair the owner's newest file actually needs.
- **The function body is reproduced whole rather than patched.** `create or replace function` replaces a body entire, so a migration showing only the changed lines would leave the current definition readable nowhere. The six changed places are each marked `019`.
- **Part 4 landed the same day and task 34 is complete.** `notificationCardCaptureSchema` carries both lists as `z.enum` over the same constant `lib/notification-card-prefill.ts` fills from, so the closed set has **one definition** rather than two that can drift; the route passes them into the RPC payload unchanged, and the form sends the two derived name lists. The schema refuses a repeated name and a `changed` that is not a subset of `offered`, and the database holds both rules again — the route is not the last boundary.
- **The ordering rule was honoured rather than reasoned about afterwards.** 019 was pushed to the hosted project and **verified against the remote before part 4 reached `main`**. The owner's backup was verified from the database rather than taken on report: sequence **27**, last-exported **27**, equal, with the newest `backup_records` row at 27 — and equal again after the push, because DDL mutates no owner data.

Pushing 019 needs its own authorization; the ordering rule binds the **push of part 4**, not this migration — 019 is safe to land on the hosted project at any time, and part 4 is not safe to deploy before it has.

Reproduced rather than assumed away, and recorded rather than quietly re-run.

## D-117 — A card is read enlarged and a slip is not, which is D-087's ladder bounded rather than reversed

- Date: 2026-08-16
- Status: **Measured and shipped.** Raised by the owner's own KBank screenshot, which settled a question two earlier guesses had got wrong.
- **What the screenshot settled.** The owner sent one of his KBank cards directly. It prints a timestamp under its title and a labelled balance row — **the grammar was right all along**, and the reader was missing rows that are plainly there. That killed the working hypothesis (a compact KBank variant printing neither) and pointed at recognition rather than at the contract.
- **Two hypotheses were tested and both failed, which is why they are recorded.** Page-segmentation mode was swept first, since missing a *whole row* looks like layout analysis discarding a region: `SINGLE_COLUMN`, `SINGLE_BLOCK` and `SPARSE_TEXT` against the automatic default scored 60, 63, 63 and 63 of 100 fields — no gain, and `SPARSE_TEXT` merely traded dates for account digits. ## D-121 — The last refused field is a tone mark misread inside a label, reproduced and not repaired

- Date: 2026-08-17
- Status: **Reproduced and diagnosed. Deliberately not fixed** — the cause is now known instead of guessed at, which is what D-119 left open, and the remedy is a grammar change that deserves its own measurement rather than a ride on an adoption commit.
- **The one field.** `S__18636806_0.jpg`, card 2, Krungthai Connext, incoming: `occurredAt` refuses as `LABEL_NOT_FOUND`. One card of 25 — every other Krungthai card on the same samples finds the same label without trouble.
- **The cause, to the code point.** The label is `วันที่ทำรายการ`. `normalise` applies NFKC, which gives `ำ` (U+0E33) its compatibility decomposition, so the label the matcher compares against holds `ท` + **U+0E4D** (nikhahit, the small circle above) + `า`. On this card Vision returns **U+0E48** (mai ek, the small stroke above) in that position. The line matches the label's first **7** characters and diverges on the 8th. So the row was read and the *label* is unfindable — not the date.
- **It is the same class as D-117's SCB slash and not the same cause, which is what D-119 suspected and could not confirm.** Both are a character carrying **no value** being misread inside a label, which makes the whole field unfindable rather than misread. The slash case was `/` read as `|`; this is one superscript Thai mark read as another that looks like it at notification size. `repairSeparators` exists for exactly the first shape.
- **Why it was not repaired here and now.** A separator repair is safe because `/` and `|` carry nothing; a Thai tone mark **does** carry meaning in ordinary text, so a rule turning U+0E48 into U+0E4D is only safe *inside a comparison against a known label* and would need bounding as carefully as `opensWith` was (D-119). It would also fire on **1 card in 25**, so the measurement that justifies it barely has a subject — the same shape of thin evidence that made D-115's month-name reader worth building, measuring at 0 of 2, and deleting the same day. Recorded so the next attempt starts from the code point rather than from the symptom.
- **What a fix would have to prove**, whenever it is attempted: that no other label on any layout becomes findable-by-accident under the same repair, and that the repair is discarded when it changes anything but a mark — the digit-guard discipline of `repairToken`, applied to glyphs.
- Evidence: a throwaway harness under `.runtime/`, run once and deleted, which printed **no line** — only the label's code points, the count of characters shared, and the single differing character. ## D-122 — An empty list is sent as an absent key, because migration 019 refuses `[]` and fails hardest when the pre-fill is perfect

- Date: 2026-08-17
- Status: **Found in production by the owner's first real card read through Cloud Vision, reproduced in the database, and fixed in the app.** The database defect is real and stays open as `PLAN.md` task 37; nothing in this app can reach it any more.
- **The symptom.** The card form filled all four digit-bearing fields correctly from Vision, the owner changed none of them, and the capture was refused with `The record of what the card pre-filled is invalid, so nothing was captured.` The reader had worked perfectly; the record of the reading is what failed.
- **The cause, reproduced rather than reasoned about.** `private.assert_prefill_field_names` (migration 019) rejects a repeated field name by comparing `array_length(v_names, 1)` against `count(distinct n)`. **PostgreSQL answers NULL, not 0, for `array_length` of an empty array**, while the count answers 0 — so `NULL is distinct from 0` is true and an empty list raises `contains a repeated field name`. Run against the local project: an **absent** key returns an empty list, `["amount"]` returns one name, and **`[]` raises**.
- **Why it fires exactly when everything works.** The form always sent both keys. `prefillChanged` is empty precisely when the owner **changed nothing** — the outcome the whole trial is hoping for. At the local engine's 70% he was always changing something, so the case never arose; Vision at 99 of 100 made it the normal one. **A defect that only appears when the feature succeeds is the kind a gate does not find**, and this one had a full green gate over it.
- **Why no test caught it, which is the more useful half.** Both the pgTAP and route tests for "no pre-fill" **omit the keys**, and omitting takes an early return that never reaches the duplicate check. Nothing anywhere sent `[]`. The two encodings look interchangeable in the migration's own prose — "absent means an empty list" — and one of them was never exercised. **Two spellings of the same intent need two tests, or the untested one is where the defect lives.**
- **The fix, and why it is not a workaround.** `namesOrAbsent` in the card form sends an absent key for an empty list. Migration 019 *defines* absent as empty and `notificationCardCaptureSchema` already marks both keys optional, so this is the contract's own encoding rather than a way around it — the audit row records `[]` either way. It needs no migration, no backup and no ordering ceremony, which is what let it ship the same hour the owner hit it.
- **The database defect stays open deliberately, as `PLAN.md` task 37.** The one-line change is `coalesce(array_length(v_names, 1), 0)`. Writing it today would open a local/hosted migration gap and demand a fresh backup that goes stale the next time a card is captured, to close a hole nothing can reach. `tests/notification-card-routes.test.ts` **holds the current refusal in place with an explicit test that flips to 201 when 020 lands** — a test is the only kind of note that fails when it goes stale.
- **What this says about the adoption it followed.** D-120 argued the pre-fill guarantees were unaffected by the engine swap, and they were: no wrong figure reached a box. What the swap changed was *which paths get exercised*, and that is the thing to look for after any accuracy jump — the success path had never been walked end to end.
- Evidence: `app/notification-card-capture.tsx` (`namesOrAbsent`), `tests/privacy.test.ts`, `tests/notification-card-routes.test.ts`, `supabase/migrations/202608160019_notification_card_prefill_audit.sql` (`private.assert_prefill_field_names`). Reproduced with three one-line queries against `private-ledger-local`; no owner data was read or written. D-120, D-116, D-114. `PLAN.md` tasks 35 and 37.

## D-127 — A label survives one misread mark, proven safe by the labels staying distinct rather than by argument

- Date: 2026-08-18
- Status: **Built and measured.** The reader now offers **112 of 112** digit-bearing fields over the real screenshots, with **no refusals at all**. This reverses D-121's "recorded rather than repaired" on better reasoning, not on new evidence about the card.
- **The defect, to the code point.** `normalise` runs NFKC, which gives `ำ` its compatibility decomposition — so `วันที่ทำรายการ` is compared as fifteen characters with a bare **U+0E4D** (nikhahit, a small circle above) at index 7. On one real Krungthai card Vision reads **U+0E48** (mai ek, a small stroke above) there: the same small shape over the same consonant. The row is read correctly and the *label* becomes unfindable, so the whole field is refused. It is D-117's slash problem in a different character class.
- **What changed my mind, and it is a reasoning error worth naming.** D-121 refused the repair because "a Thai tone mark carries meaning where a slash does not". That is true of **general text** and this comparison is never made against general text — it is made against a **closed set of labels** written down in `lib/notification-card.ts`. I had applied a rule about prose to a lookup against a fixed vocabulary, which made a checkable question look like a judgement call.
- **So it became a check.** `labels are distinct under mark tolerance` enumerates every label in the **real** field maps, across both directions of all three layouts, and asserts no two of them differ only by marks above the line. If that ever fails the tolerance must go, and the test says so. **That is the difference between this and D-115's month-name reader**: that one was built on thin evidence about the world, and this one rests on a property of a table this repository owns.
- **Bounded three ways, and the bounds are the design.** Only characters that are **both** Thai marks *above* the line may differ (below-vowels U+0E38–U+0E3A are excluded, because the observed confusion is between shapes drawn above a consonant); **at most one** such substitution across a whole label; and lengths must still agree. A consonant, a digit or a below-vowel differing means a different row, however small the difference looks — both asserted.
- **The prefix check had to move with the completion check.** `labelAtLineStart` accumulates words and abandons a row as soon as it diverges. Relaxing only the completion test would have abandoned the row *at the misread mark* and never reached the relaxed test at all — so `labelStillPossible` is tolerant too. A half-applied tolerance would have looked correct and done nothing.
- **It cannot reach a value.** A label match says only where a field's value begins; every digit still passes `parseThb`, the digit guard and blank-on-failure downstream (D-114). The worst a wrong label match could do is aim a crop at the wrong row, which is what the distinctness proof rules out.
- **Measured through the shipped path**, not a copy of its arithmetic: over the real screenshots the reader now offers **amount, balance, timestamp and account digits at 28/28 each — 112 of 112, no refusal of any kind**, against 99 of 100 before. The sample has grown to 13 images and 28 cards as the owner captures more.
- **`MAX_MARK_SUBSTITUTIONS` is 1 and is not a tuning knob.** A second substitution has never been observed and each one multiplies the chance two labels meet in the middle. Raising it means re-running the distinctness proof, and the constant's comment says so.
- Evidence: `lib/notification-card-ocr.ts` (`markTolerantCompare`, `labelReached`, `labelStillPossible`, `THAI_MARK_ABOVE`), `tests/notification-card-ocr.test.ts` § a label survives one misread mark.
